SEO Basics for a New Website

Educational article · 9 min read · Updated 8 November 2026

← Back to all guides

SEO & BLOGGING · 9 min read · Growth Hardik · Educational resource · Updated 8 November 2026

Search engine optimisation (SEO) is the work of making a website easier for search engines to discover and understand, while making its pages useful to people looking for relevant information. SEO is not a one-time setting, a shortcut or a guarantee of first-page rankings. It combines helpful content, clear site structure and technical accessibility over time.

This guide focuses on practical foundations for a small or new website. You do not need to apply every advanced tactic at once; start by making the most important pages clear, useful and easy to reach.

1. Understand search intent

Search intent is the need behind a query. Someone searching “what is SEO” may want a beginner explanation, while someone searching for a local repair service may be comparing providers. A page should answer the question its title promises to answer.

Write down the questions your intended readers ask before creating a page. Use customer conversations, support questions and your own subject knowledge to identify useful topics. Search suggestions can provide ideas, but do not create pages just to repeat keywords.

2. Publish original, helpful content

A useful page gives readers information they can understand and act on. Explain concepts in your own words, include relevant examples, disclose limitations and check factual claims. Where you refer to research or platform instructions, link to the original source.

Avoid copying competitors, lightly rewriting existing articles or publishing many near-identical pages. A short page can be useful when it fully answers a narrow question; a long page is not automatically better. The goal is to satisfy the reader’s need, not hit an arbitrary word count.

3. Write descriptive page titles and descriptions

Give each important page a unique title that accurately describes its main topic. Keep it readable and avoid repeating keywords unnaturally. A meta description can summarise the page and help searchers understand what they may find, although search engines may generate a different snippet.

For example, “SEO Basics for a New Website” is more informative than a vague title such as “Welcome”. Do not promise rankings or results the page cannot deliver.

4. Organise headings and page structure

Use one clear main heading for the page and organise the content into logical sections with descriptive subheadings. Headings should describe the section that follows, not be used only to make text look large. Write short paragraphs and use lists where they make instructions easier to follow.

5. Build useful internal links

Internal links help visitors move between related pages and help crawlers discover content. Link from a relevant guide to a deeper explanation, from a service page to a contact page, and from articles back to the blog index. Use descriptive link text so readers know what they will open.

Check that links point to the correct destination and do not lead to missing pages. Avoid adding links that do not help the reader simply to increase the number of links on a page.

6. Make the website technically accessible

  • Use a mobile-friendly layout with readable text and tap-friendly navigation.
  • Keep important content available as text rather than embedding everything in images.
  • Use descriptive file names and meaningful alternative text for informative images.
  • Compress large images and remove scripts or styles that are not needed.
  • Use HTTPS and keep navigation and page URLs consistent.
  • Set an accurate canonical URL for pages that may have duplicate URL versions.
  • Fix broken internal links and avoid accidental noindex directives on pages meant to appear in search.

7. Help search engines discover your pages

A sitemap can list the canonical URLs you want search engines to discover. Keep it accurate and update it when important pages are added or removed. A robots.txt file gives crawler instructions, but it is not a way to remove a page from search results. Do not block resources that search engines need to render a page.

Google Search Console can help website owners inspect indexing, submit a sitemap and review search performance. Verify the correct property, inspect important URLs and read any reported issue. Submitting a URL or sitemap does not guarantee crawling, indexing or ranking.

8. Measure progress without chasing vanity metrics

Search Console reports queries, impressions, clicks and average position for eligible search data. Review which pages receive impressions but few clicks, and whether the page actually answers the query. Use analytics responsibly if installed, and make sure your privacy notice reflects the tracking tools in use.

Search performance can take time to change. Compare similar date ranges and consider seasonality, changes in demand and website updates. Avoid making repeated title changes based on a handful of impressions.

A practical new-site SEO checklist

  1. List the important pages and the purpose of each one.
  2. Write original content that answers real audience questions.
  3. Add a unique title, useful description and one clear H1 to each key page.
  4. Link related pages together using meaningful anchor text.
  5. Check mobile usability, loading performance and HTTPS.
  6. Review canonical URLs, sitemap.xml and robots.txt.
  7. Test internal links and remove placeholder or duplicate pages.
  8. Verify the site in Search Console and review indexing reports regularly.

Conclusion

Strong SEO starts with a website that deserves to be found: clear, useful pages supported by accessible technical foundations. Focus on readers first, help search engines understand the site and use Search Console data to make measured improvements. No checklist can guarantee a ranking, but these fundamentals create a sound starting point.

Editorial note: Search systems and documentation evolve. Treat this as a starting guide and consult current official Google Search Central documentation for implementation details.
